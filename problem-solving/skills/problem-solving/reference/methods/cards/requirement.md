# Card: what is wanted, how the result is counted, where the edges are

**Use when** the problem is described in someone else's words ("too slow", "not what I asked"), a target arrives from above, or people argue about whether something is "late" or "wrong". **Skip** when the requirement is already written and agreed — point to it.

**Needs** whoever the work is for (customer, boss, the person who asked, the next step in the chain) and their own words.
**Gives** a one-line result measure, a requirement with its source, and the boundaries.

## Steps
1. **Their words, quoted.** Write what the person the work is for actually said or wrote, with where it came from. Do not use your paraphrase or a colleague's summary as the yardstick.
2. **From words to something you can count** (voice of the customer → what matters most → a measure → a requirement):
   - what they care about ("orders arrive when promised")
   - what drives it ("ship date", "address correct")
   - the measure ("days from order to delivery, per order")
   - the requirement and who set it ("≤ 3 working days, contract clause 4") — if nobody set one, say so; do not invent it.
3. **Map the edges** with a five-column line (suppliers → inputs → process → outputs → customers): where the problem starts and ends, what is in scope and what is not, what must not get worse.
4. **Who decides** changes inside those edges, and what needs someone else.

## Checks
- If two people's words conflict in a way that changes the fix, show both and ask; don't pick.
- A target from above ("save 8%") is cascaded to the biggest slice first (`../selector.md` A, `../../../rules/problem-framing.md`).
