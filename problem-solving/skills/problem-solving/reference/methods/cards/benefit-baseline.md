# Card: record the starting point — benefit and burden

**Use when** a change is about to be tried and anyone will later ask "was it worth it?". **Skip** for problems where nobody will weigh cost.

**Needs** the volume of work and today's numbers for time, errors, rework and spend.
**Gives** a before-record now, and later an after-record on the same basis.

## Before the change (same period length as you will compare later)
- Volume handled (orders, tickets, documents).
- Time: waiting and working, from records or a timed sample.
- Errors and rework: rate, not count.
- Spend that could change: overtime, outside services, material, refunds.
- Burden on people: approvals, checks, reading, chasing.

## After — keep these apart
| Kind | What it means | Not the same as |
|---|---|---|
| Observed change | measured on the same basis as before | proof that the change caused it |
| Time freed | hours that can go to other work | cash — only if spend actually falls |
| Spend actually reduced | money that stopped being spent | money expected to be avoided later |
| New work the change adds | checking, reruns, exceptions, maintenance, licences | — subtract it |

- Same freed hours are not counted twice (as savings and as new output).
- Volume falling can make totals fall: compare rates.
- A range of assumptions is not a confidence interval; without a basis, give the break-even condition instead ("worth it if errors fall below …").
- Numbers come from the scripts or the records, never from estimates in prose.

Sources: HM Government, "The Government Efficiency Framework" (cash-releasing vs non-cash-releasing gains; baselines; costs of realising benefits) — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-government-efficiency-framework/the-government-efficiency-framework--2 (OGL v3.0)
