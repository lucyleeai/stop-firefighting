The expert move: the business target IS the problem statement once it is cascaded. Break the $2M down (Pareto of addressable spend by category/supplier/part), pick the area where the gap is largest, define a measurable output (e.g. unit price, total cost of ownership, price variance vs index) and its drivers (Y = f(x)); recognise that a supplier's price is our input (x) but is the output (y) of the supplier's own process, so some savings come from working on the supplier's drivers (volume, specs, logistics), not just negotiation.
PASS only if it (a) turns the savings target into a defined problem by cascading/decomposing it with data (e.g. a Pareto of spend) down to a specific scope and measurable output, and (b) mentions looking at the cost drivers behind price (specs, volumes, logistics, the supplier's process), not only negotiating harder.
FAIL if it only gives a generic DMAIC phase description or a list of generic savings levers without the cascade-to-a-defined-problem step.
