PASS only if ALL hold:
- It says a before/after difference in a rate needs the underlying counts (number of packs and errors in each period) to judge whether 2.1% vs 1.4% is more than chance — e.g. a two-proportion comparison — and does not claim significance without those counts.
- It raises at least one other threat to the comparison: the trial period is short, other things may have changed (volume, product mix, staff, attention on the trial line), or a before/after trend should be plotted over time.
- It proposes a sensible path to roll-out (e.g. extend or replicate on another line, measure the same way, then standardise with a control/sustain step) rather than a plain yes/no.
FAIL if it simply approves or rejects the roll-out, or states the improvement is statistically significant without counts.
